# Body topics people rarely talk about (Nov 7 2026, round 2)

> **Information, not medical advice.** This file collects normal ranges, published links, what helps and warning signs, so MeMap's body questions can help honestly. Copy in the app should say "talk to a doctor", never "you have X".

Numbers in brackets point to `sources.md` (round 2 = 268–448; round 1 numbers are reused where the same source applies).

The proposed questions are in `body-proposed-additions.md`. It also has the red-flag list ("don't count, act tonight").

## How to read this file

- **Evidence**
  - **strong** = guideline "strong" recommendation, or a high-quality meta-analysis of RCTs.
  - **moderate** = RCTs with limits, or consistent cohorts.
  - **low** = small trials, cross-sectional data, lab physiology.
  - **advice** = patient pages and expert advice, no trial found.
  - **mixed** = studies disagree.
- **Lag**
  - **same day** = the factor and the symptom on the same evening's answers.
  - **night** = an evening factor and a "last night" question answered the next evening.
  - **next day** = the symptom the day after.
  - **weeks / months** = too slow for day-level links.
- **Access.** nice.org.uk, cks.nice.org.uk and mayoclinic.org returned 403 in this round. NICE items come from NHS pages, reproductions or search snippets and are tagged that way in `sources.md`. Check NICE wording before putting it on a screen.

## 1. Bowel: how often, how hard, how loose

### What is normal
- **Frequency**
  - Between 3 a day and 3 a week covers almost everyone:
    - 98% of healthy adults in a 7-day diary study [268]
    - 95.9% of 4,775 US adults [269]
  - Once a day is the most common habit, but only a minority have it:
    - A regular 24-hour rhythm: 40% of men, 33% of women.
    - A third of women go less than daily [270].
  - **A day without a poo is normal.**
- **Stool form (healthy adults)**
  - 77% normal, 12% hard, 10% loose [268].
  - The usual range on the Bristol scale: types 3–5 for men, 2–6 for women [269].
  - The Bristol scale is valid (κ 0.78); types 2 and 3 get confused most [274].
- **Common in healthy people "sometimes"**
  - Straining: 47%.
  - Feeling not fully emptied: 46%.
  - Urgency: 36% [268].
  - So one hard day means nothing; a pattern does.
- **Constipation (Rome IV) [271]**
  - Two or more of these, on more than 1 in 4 poos:
    - straining
    - hard or lumpy stool
    - feeling not emptied
    - feeling blocked
    - needing manual help
  - **Or** fewer than 3 poos a week without laxatives.
  - The pattern must hold for 3 months, with onset at least 6 months before.
  - About 10% of people meet it worldwide [272].
- **Diarrhoea**
  - WHO: 3 or more loose or watery stools in 24 hours [275].
  - Functional diarrhoea: loose stools in more than 1 in 4 poos [271].
  - A stomach bug usually stops within 5–7 days [300].
- **Bloating**
  - Rome IV counts it in days a week: at least 1 day a week, for 3 months [271].
- **IBS (Rome IV)**
  - Belly pain on at least 1 day a week.
  - Plus 2 of these 3: linked to pooing, a change in how often, a change in form.
  - 4.1% (online survey) or 1.5% (household survey) [271, 273].
- **Wind**
  - About 10 times a day on average; up to 20 is normal [276].

### Memory is a poor witness: a diary fixes that
- 16% recalled their weekly frequency wrongly by 3 or more [304].
- Only half of people who said they were constipated were, by a 4-week diary. The rest averaged about 6 poos a week [305].
- Recalled and diary IBS type agreed in only 54% of people [306].
- Recall exaggerated symptoms compared with 7-day app check-ins [307].
- Standard diaries run 7 days, 2 weeks or 4 weeks [268, 304, 305].

**This is the strongest argument for MeMap here:** a daily answer is more trustworthy than memory.

### What goes with it

| Factor | Direction | Lag | Evidence | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Psyllium (fibre supplement) | More and softer poos | 4+ weeks | moderate (RCT meta-analysis); guideline conditional | 278, 279 |

### What helps

**Constipation**
- Psyllium, at least 10 g a day for at least 4 weeks: 66% respond vs 41% without [279]. Of the fibres, only psyllium had good evidence (+2.3 poos a week). Wheat bran and inulin are weak, and inulin adds gas [278].
- Two kiwis a day or 100 g of prunes: as good as psyllium. Kiwi had the fewest side effects [280, 282].
  - The British Dietetic Association guideline lists fibre supplements, kiwi, prunes, rye bread and high-mineral water [281].
- Water: helps people who drink little [278, 283].
- Laxatives (AGA–ACG):
  - PEG (macrogol): strong.
  - Bisacodyl / picosulfate short-term: strong.
  - Senna: conditional.
  - Magnesium oxide: conditional, very low [278].
- Regular toilet time, not ignoring the urge: NHS advice, no trial found [300].

**Bloating and IBS**
- Low-FODMAP diet: the top-ranked diet. It beat standard diet advice for bloating (RR 0.72) [298]. ACG suggests a time-limited trial with professional help [297].
- Peppermint oil: helps 1 in 4 more than placebo (NNT 4) [299].
- Soluble fibre [297].
- NHS self-help [300]:
  - oats
  - up to 1 tablespoon of linseed a day
  - no more than 3 portions of fruit a day
  - no more than 3 cups of tea or coffee a day
  - avoid sorbitol
  - try a probiotic for 4 weeks

### Red flags
- **Tonight:**
  - black or dark-red poo
  - bloody diarrhoea
  - constipation with vomiting, not passing wind, or constant pain
  - diarrhoea with signs of dehydration, a high fever, or 6+ loose stools a day
  - [277, 300]
- **Soon (book a doctor, don't count):**
  - blood in poo, or poo softer or thinner, for 3 weeks
  - weight loss without trying
  - diarrhoea for more than 7 days (NHS; NIDDK says more than 2 days)
  - IBS-type symptoms for over 4 weeks
  - waking at night with diarrhoea
  - new bowel change at 50+
  - a family history of bowel cancer or IBD
  - [277, 297, 300]
- **NICE NG12 (via a reproduction) [301]:**
  - 40+: weight loss with belly pain
  - 50+: unexplained rectal bleeding
  - 60+: a change in bowel habit or iron-deficiency anaemia
  - A FIT stool test now gates many referrals [301].

### Ask daily?
- **Yes**, but read the answers as a week, not a day.
  - One "no poo" day is normal and should not look like a hard day.
  - "Poo on 2 or fewer days of 7" is a fair stand-in for Rome's "fewer than 3 a week".
  - "Hard to go" and "loose" are good day-level stand-ins for Rome's per-poo items.
- Don't ask people to count wind.
- Don't ask for a Bristol number every evening. Hard / normal / loose is enough for a yes/no app.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** a 4-week diary that a doctor trusts more than memory. The person's own links (travel, period days, alcohol, stress the same day, a new medicine). Kiwi or psyllium tries over 4 weeks. It cannot say "IBS" or rule out bowel disease. Blood or black poo always goes to a doctor.

## 2. Piles (haemorrhoids)

- **Normal**
  - Found in 39% of adults at colonoscopy; 55% of them have no symptoms [302].
- **Goes with it**
  - Constipation, straining, heavy lifting, pregnancy, age, being overweight (advice level) [300].
  - Alcohol and caffeine are listed by the NHS as adding to constipation [300].
- **What helps**
  - Fibre halves lasting symptoms (RR 0.53) and bleeding (RR 0.50). It has no clear effect on prolapse, pain or itch (moderate, 7 RCTs) [303].
  - NHS [300]:
    - Don't strain, and don't sit on the toilet longer than needed.
    - Don't ignore the urge.
    - Warm baths, an ice pack.
    - Avoid codeine; no ibuprofen if bleeding.
- **Red flags [300]**
  - Painless bleeding from the bottom → urgent GP or 111.
  - High temperature or pus → urgent GP.
  - Non-stop bleeding, a lot of blood or big clots, severe pain → A&E.
  - Not better after 7 days of home care, or keeps coming back → GP.
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes, as "bothered today?", for people who opt in.
  - Never as "did you see blood?" every evening: bleeding is a red flag, not a count.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** a link with hard-to-poop days, a fibre try, and the NHS 7-day rule as the action point.

## 3. Heartburn and reflux (and how to keep stomach pain and nausea apart)

- **Normal**
  - Occasional heartburn is common.
  - Weekly heartburn [311]:
    - North America: 18–28%
    - Europe: 9–26%
    - Middle East: 8.7–33.1%
    - East Asia: 2.5–7.8%
  - Reflux becomes a disease when symptoms are "troublesome" (Montreal) [309]. In studies that usually means mild symptoms on 2+ days a week, or moderate–severe on 1+ day a week [310].
  - NHS: heartburn on most days → see a GP [316].

### What goes with it

| Factor | Direction | Lag | Evidence | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dinner less than 3 h before bed | ↑ (OR 7.45 vs 4+ h) | Same night | low (case-control, small RCT); ACG conditional | 308, 312, 313 |
| Being overweight / gaining weight | ↑ | Months | moderate; ACG strong | 308, 313 |
| Smoking | ↑ | Months | low | 308, 313 |
| Alcohol | ↑ | Same evening | low (lab) | 308, 316 |
| Coffee | Small or none | Same day | **mixed**: one meta-analysis null, another OR 1.18 with high heterogeneity | 314, 315 |
| Fatty or large meals, chocolate, peppermint | ↑ | Same evening | low (lab only) | 308 |
| Sleeping on the right side | ↑ night reflux | Night | low (physiology) | 308 |
| Pregnancy, stress, ibuprofen-type painkillers, tight waistbands | ↑ | Varies | advice | 316 |

- **No trial of cutting out single foods** met the review criteria [313].
- ACG now advises avoiding the foods that trigger *you* (conditional, low) [308]. That is exactly what a personal diary finds.

### What helps
- Weight loss if overweight: ACG strong, moderate evidence [308].
- Last meal 2–3 h before bed (NHS says 3–4 h): conditional, low [308, 316].
- Raise the head of the bed by 10–20 cm with blocks or a wedge, **not pillows**: conditional, low. A small RCT showed less night acid [308, 313, 316].
- Stop smoking; loose waistbands [308, 316].
- Antacids when needed. An 8-week PPI trial, if a doctor agrees and there are no alarm signs: strong [308].

### Red flags
- **Tonight (999)**:
  - chest pain that spreads to the arm, jaw, neck or back, or comes with sweating, breathlessness or sickness. A heart attack can feel like indigestion [317].
  - vomiting blood, or vomit like coffee grounds [318]
  - black sticky poo [318]
  - sudden severe belly pain [318]
- **Soon:**
  - food sticking or trouble swallowing, at any age
  - weight loss
  - frequent vomiting
  - 55+ with new heartburn or indigestion plus weight loss
  - heartburn most days despite changes
  - [308, 316, 319]

### Stomach pain, nausea and heartburn are three questions, not one
- **Functional dyspepsia (Rome IV)** [320, 321]:
  - fullness after meals
  - early fullness
  - pain or burning in the upper belly
  - It overlaps with reflux in up to 40% of patients [321].
- **Keep them apart in the wording:**
  - heartburn = burning rising behind the breastbone, or a sour taste in the throat
  - stomach pain = pain in the belly
  - nausea = feeling sick
- They will often come together. Show that as "often come together", not as a discovery.

### Ask daily?
- **Yes**: heartburn is a clear, memorable event, and its definition is already "days a week".
- Ask about "today or last night", so night heartburn after a late dinner is caught.
- The existing `ate-late` ("big meal within 2 hours of bed") is the natural partner.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** find the person's own triggers. Generic food lists are weakly supported, and no elimination trial exists. The late-dinner link is the one most likely to show up. Then run a 2-week "last meal 3 hours before bed" try.

## 4. Bladder: night peeing, urgency, burning, leaking

### Night peeing (nocturia)
- **Definition**
  - Waking to pee during the main sleep [322].
- **Normal**
  - Healthy women average 0.4 night pees (range 0–2) [331].
  - **Once a night is not different from none** for quality of life. It drops at 2, and more at 3+ [324].
  - Share getting up 2+ times [323]:
    - age 20–40: 2–18%
    - over 70: 28–62%

| Factor | Direction | Lag | Evidence | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drinks in the 2 h before bed, especially caffeine or alcohol | ↑ | Night | advice (NHS board guideline) | 325, 327 |
| Evening diuretic tablets | ↑ | Night | advice | 325 |
| Leg swelling, heart failure, sleep apnoea (night-time urine production) | ↑ | Chronic | guideline | 325, 326 |
| Diabetes, enlarged prostate, overactive bladder, UTI, menopause | ↑ | Chronic | advice | 327 |
| 2+ night pees → worse sleep and next-day tiredness | ↓ sleep | Next day | moderate | 324, 325 |

- **What helps**
  - Stop drinks 2 h before bed; under 2 L a day; empty the bladder before bed; less salt [325].
  - If ankles swell: raise the legs for a few hours before bed [325].
  - Take diuretics mid-afternoon [325].
  - Treating sleep apnoea with CPAP cut about 1 night pee (low: high heterogeneity) [329].
- **Red flags**
  - Urgent: blood in urine, pain, fever, can't pee [318, 328].
  - Soon: no obvious cause, excess thirst, tiredness, a change in pattern, snoring or gasping [325–328].
  - The EAU says even non-bothersome nocturia can merit a check [326].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes, as **"2 or more times last night"**. Plain "got up to pee" would mark normal nights as hard [324].
  - Diary answers for nocturia agree well with other measures (κ 0.65) [333].

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** the evening-drink link (alcohol, late coffee), the link with next-day tiredness, a 2-week "no drinks 2 hours before bed" try. If it doesn't help, a doctor check (including for sleep apnoea).

### Urgency (overactive bladder)
- **Normal**
  - 4–10 pees in the day, 4–12 in 24 h; average 7 [331]. Medians 7–8 [332].
  - "Up to 7 in the day" is a tradition, not a hard line [330].
- **What helps**
  - Bladder training, slowly stretching the time between pees: AUA strong, Grade A [330]; at least 6 weeks [339].
  - Less caffeine and alcohol; fluid management: clinical principle [330].
  - Weight loss if BMI over 30 [340].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Weakly. Urgency is the least reliable diary item (κ −0.22) [333].
  - Ask about a concrete event: "a sudden urge that was hard to hold".

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** a 6-week bladder-training try with a count of urgent days. Not a diagnosis.

### Burning when peeing (UTI signs)
- **Symptoms**
  - Burning, cloudy pee, needing to go often, lower belly or back pain, fever [334].
- **Recurrent UTI**
  - 2 in 6 months, or 3 in 12 months [335, 338].
- **What helps prevent it**
  - +1.5 L of water a day in women who drank under 1.5 L: 1.7 vs 3.2 infections a year (open-label RCT, industry-funded) [336]. EAU: weak recommendation [335].
  - Cranberry: RR 0.70 overall, 0.74 in women with recurrent UTI (moderate, Cochrane). Not shown for pregnancy or older people in care [337].
  - Peeing after sex, wiping front to back: advised, but the evidence is limited [334, 335].
  - Vaginal oestrogen after menopause: strong [335].
- **Red flags [334]**
  - Confusion or drowsiness → 999.
  - Fever, shivering, back or side pain, blood in urine → same day.
  - Men, pregnancy, 65+, diabetes, a catheter → same day.
  - Not better within 48 h → GP.
- **Ask daily?**
  - Only as a safety net and an episode counter.
  - Infections come a few times a year: too few for honest links.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** the right same-day step, a 48-hour nudge, and a "2 in 6 months" recurrence note for the doctor.

### Leaking (urinary incontinence)
- **Normal**
  - Common but **not normal**: 25–45% of women, over 40% at 70+ [341].
  - The NHS says it is not an unavoidable part of ageing [339].
  - Only 28% of women with leaking seek help (24% at 20–50) [343].
- **Goes with it**
  - Stress leaking (cough, sneeze, lift): childbirth, weight.
  - Urge leaking: overactive bladder, caffeine (advice) [339].
  - Mostly slow, not day to day. Caffeine and fluids act the same day.
- **What helps**
  - **Pelvic floor training**: stress leaking cured in 56% vs 6% (high-quality Cochrane) [342].
    - NHS: at least 8 squeezes, 3 times a day, for at least 3 months [339].
    - NICE: supervised, for at least 3 months, first line [340].
    - An app-guided programme worked in an RCT [437].
  - Bladder training for urge leaking: at least 6 weeks [339].
  - Cut caffeine. Don't cut fluids too far; that can make it worse [339].
  - Weight loss [340].
- **Red flags**
  - See a GP for any leaking [339].
  - Urgent: leaking with fever, blood, or a sudden loss of control [328].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes: leak days per week. Diary agreement for leaking is moderate (κ 0.35) [333].
  - Pair it with "did the exercises today?", so the 3-month progress is visible.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** this is one of the best fits in the whole library. The problem is common and rarely told, the fix is proven, simple and slow, and progress is hard to feel without a record. The best-studied app worked through reminders [438].

## 5. Heavy period days

- **Normal**
  - NICE defines heavy bleeding by its **impact on life**, not by millilitres [348].
  - Practical signs [345]:
    - changing every 1–2 hours
    - using two products at once
    - bleeding through clothes or bedding
    - clots bigger than about 2.5 cm
    - a period longer than 7 days
  - 27% of women report 2 or more signs; 46% of them never saw a doctor [344].
- **Goes with it**
  - Iron deficiency → tiredness and breathlessness over months. 63% of women with heavy periods in one survey had been told they had iron deficiency or anaemia [344, 345].
  - Fibroids, endometriosis, adenomyosis, bleeding disorders, blood thinners [345].
  - Heavier bleeding can come in perimenopause [353].
- **What helps**
  - Hormonal coil (LNG-IUS): best, low certainty.
  - Tranexamic acid: moderate certainty.
  - NSAIDs: a slight reduction [347].
  - An iron blood test [345].
- **Red flags**
  - **Tonight**: soaking through 1–2 pads or tampons an hour for 2+ hours, or bleeding with faintness or breathlessness [346].
  - **Soon**:
    - bleeding between periods or after sex
    - any bleeding after menopause
    - heavy bleeding with pain when peeing, pooing or during sex
    - clots on more than one day
    - [345, 346, 353]
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes, but only on period days, as a follow-up to `period-day`. Count heavy days per period.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** count heavy days per cycle and link them with tiredness, dizziness and low energy. A two-cycle summary for a GP helps, because NICE judges heavy bleeding by its impact and a diary shows that impact.

## 6. Hot flushes and night sweats

- **Normal**
  - Up to 80% of women in the menopause transition have hot flushes [350].
  - They last a median of 7.4 years, and more than 11.8 years if they start early in perimenopause [349]. The NHS says 7–9 years [353].
- **Goes with it**

| Factor | Direction | Lag | Evidence | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alcohol | **Mixed**: monthly drinkers had fewer flushes in one study; only ≥40 g a day raised new flushes in another | Same day / night | mixed | 356, 357, 350 |
| Caffeine | Slightly more | Same day | low (cross-sectional) | 355 |
| Hot room, rising body heat | Can set one off | Minutes | low; no trial shows cooling cuts their number | 350 |
| Higher weight | More flushes, early in the transition | Years | low–moderate | 350 |
| Night sweats → broken sleep | ↓ sleep, ↑ tiredness | Next day | moderate | 353 |

- **NAMS rates "avoid your triggers" as not recommended**, because no trial supports it [350]. MeMap can still show a person's *own* link, labelled as theirs, but must not promise that general trigger lists work.
- **What helps**
  - Hormone therapy is the most effective [351, 352].
  - CBT and clinical hypnosis: Level I [350]. In one hypnosis RCT, flushes fell 74% vs 17%.
  - Non-hormone medicines a doctor can offer: fezolinetant, low-dose paroxetine, other SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin [350].
  - Not recommended for flushes [350]:
    - paced breathing
    - supplements
    - yoga or exercise as a treatment
    - cooling
    - trigger avoidance
  - **Placebo improvement is 20–66%** in these trials [350]. Anything tried will often look like it works at first.
- **Red flags**
  - Soon: night sweats with fever or shivering, cough, diarrhoea or weight loss [354].
  - Soon: regular soaking sweats that wake you [354].
  - Soon: any bleeding after menopause [353].
- **Ask daily?**
  - For frequent flushers, "any hot flush today?" is yes every day; trial entry needed 7+ a day [358].
  - Ask **"did hot flushes bother you today?"** instead, plus a separate **"woke up sweating last night?"**.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** show how many days flushes bothered you and how night sweats tie to sleep and next-day energy. That turns a vague "it's bad" into numbers for a GP, which is the main thing women value in menopause apps [443]. Point to treatments that work (HRT, CBT), not to breathing tricks.

## 7. Vaginal dryness or soreness (sensitive)

- **Normal**
  - 27–84% of women after menopause have genitourinary symptoms; often not diagnosed [359].
  - Also with breastfeeding, pregnancy, hormonal contraception, antidepressants, cancer treatment, douching [360].
- **Goes with it**
  - Pain during sex: OR 7.9 [366].
  - Perfumed products [360].
- **What helps**
  - Lubricants and moisturisers for mild cases [359, 360].
  - Vaginal oestrogen, DHEA or ospemifene for moderate–severe, including for women already on HRT [359, 351, 352].
  - Laser: not enough evidence [359].
- **Red flags (soon) [360]**
  - bleeding after sex, between periods or after menopause
  - unusual discharge
  - lasting more than a few weeks despite self-care
- **Ask daily?**
  - Weak, because it changes slowly. Its value is the action point, not links.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** a private, low-effort nudge toward a treatment most women never get offered.

## 8. Breast tenderness

- **Normal**
  - 68–69% of women have cyclical breast symptoms; 11–22% moderate to severe [362].
  - It usually comes in the 2 weeks before a period, in both breasts [361].
- **Goes with it**
  - Luteal phase: strong [361].
  - Hormonal contraception, HRT, some antidepressants [361].
  - Caffeine: **mixed**, old studies split [365].
  - In one survey, 48% of women with this pain said it got in the way of sex [362].
- **What helps**
  - A well-fitting supportive bra plus reassurance helps most [364, 361].
  - Pain-relief gel (NSAID) if pain persists: relief in 70–92% [364].
  - Evening primrose oil: no better than placebo [363].
- **Red flags**
  - Urgent: pain with fever, or a hot, swollen or discoloured area [361].
  - Soon [361]:
    - a new lump
    - nipple discharge (bloody, or one side)
    - dimpled skin
    - pain that is new, one-sided or doesn't follow the cycle
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes; it follows the cycle well.
  - But `pms-signs` already mentions sore breasts. Offer `breast-tender` only to people without `pms-signs`, or on the pill or HRT.

## 9. Sexual health (sensitive)

### Pain during sex
- **Normal**
  - 7.5% of sexually active UK women had pain for 3+ months in the past year; about a quarter of them severe [366].
  - 24% at 18 months after giving birth. Emergency caesarean and vacuum birth roughly double the odds [367].
- **Goes with it [366]**
  - dryness: OR 7.9
  - anxiety about sex: OR 6.3
  - not enjoying sex: OR 6.1
  - depressive symptoms: OR 1.7
  - Deep pain → possible endometriosis [369].
- **What helps**
  - Lubricants.
  - Vaginal oestrogen if menopausal.
  - Pelvic floor physiotherapy (a mainstay).
  - CBT, dilators [368].
- **Red flags**
  - Soon: recurring pain [368].
  - Soon: pain with bleeding after sex [345, 360].
  - Soon: deep pain with very painful periods [369].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Only on days with sex (follow-up to `had-sex`). Asked every day, "no sex" would read as "no pain".

### Erection trouble
- **Normal**
  - 52% of men aged 40–70 have some degree. Complete ED rises from 5% to 15% between 40 and 70 [370].
  - Occasional trouble from stress, tiredness or heavy drinking is common [377].
- **Goes with it**
  - **Heart disease**: ED raises the risk of cardiovascular events (RR about 1.45) [372].
    - In men with coronary disease who also had ED, it came first in 93%, about 2 years earlier [373].
    - It is a strong early warning.
  - Blood pressure, diabetes, depression; smoking in men with heart disease [370].
  - Severe sleep apnoea: ED in 72%. CPAP helped in a small study [376].
  - Heavy drinking → occasional ED [377]. Regular light drinkers look better in surveys, but that is probably confounding [375].
- **What helps**
  - Lifestyle and weight loss: normal function in 31% vs 5% after 2 years (RCT) [374].
  - PDE5 tablets [371, 377].
  - Sex therapy [377].
- **Red flags**
  - Erection lasting over 3–4 hours → A&E [377].
  - Keeps happening → GP, including heart, blood-pressure and sugar checks [371, 377].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Only on sex or attempt days.
  - **The action point matters more than links**: "keeps happening → check your heart health".

### Low desire
- **Normal**
  - 43% of US women report some sexual problem; only 12% are distressed by it [378].
  - The clinical line is 6 months plus distress [368]. Day-to-day swings are normal [381].
- **Goes with it**
  - One more hour of sleep → more desire next day and OR 1.14 for sex next day [380].
  - Stress and desire push each other down (14-day diary). Another diary study found daily hassles didn't matter; closeness did. Mixed [381].
  - A partner's desire and relationship quality [381].
  - Desire rises around ovulation [381].
  - SSRIs, menopause, breastfeeding, depression, painful sex [368].
  - Hormonal pill: most women unaffected [382].
- **What helps**
  - First: education and fixing modifiable factors.
  - Then: sex therapy, CBT or mindfulness.
  - Then: medicines [379].
  - Ask the doctor about a medicine switch if it started with an antidepressant [368].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes. A daily sex log is reliable (ICC about 0.8) [381].
  - Never call a few low days a problem.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** private links with sleep, stress and closeness. For ED, a respectful prompt to check heart health. For pain, the reassurance that it is common, has causes and is treatable, and a doctor route.

## 10. Skin: breakouts and itchy flares

### Acne breakouts
- **Normal**
  - Adult prevalence was not confirmed from an opened source (a snippet gave 41% of women aged 25–40).
  - A premenstrual flare affects 44% (survey of 400) to 65% (clinic sample) of women with acne [383, 384]. It starts up to 7 days before the period and clears about a week after [384].
- **Goes with it**

| Factor | Direction | Lag | Evidence | Src |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week before the period | ↑ | Cycle phase | moderate (surveys) | 383, 384 |
| Stress (exams) | ↑ (r 0.61) | Same weeks | low (n=22) | 389 |
| Dairy | ↑ (OR 1.25) | Weeks | low (observational, biased); AAD: too little to advise diet | 388, 385, 386 |
| Picking, greasy products, some medicines, PCOS | ↑ | Days–weeks | advice | 390 |

- **What helps**
  - Benzoyl peroxide, topical retinoids or combinations: strong (AAD) [385].
  - Judge a treatment after **12 weeks** (NICE) [387].
  - Gentle washing, no picking [390].
- **Red flags**
  - Soon: deep, painful or scarring spots; infection; mood affected; self-care failed [390].
  - Nothing tonight.
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes, as "new spots today".
  - Links need a **lag of 1–7 days** and the cycle, not the same day.
  - Don't build food-guilt links.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** show whether your breakouts follow your cycle. Help you stick with one treatment for 12 weeks instead of switching every week.

### Eczema and itchy flares (fits the existing `skin-allergy`)
- **Normal**
  - Adults: 2.1–4.9% across countries [391]; 7.3% in the US [392].
  - About 1 in 5 people get chronic itch at some point [397].
- **Goes with it**
  - Past-week itch triggers in 587 adults [393]:
    - stress: 35%
    - sweat: 31%
    - weather change: 25%
    - dry air: 24%
    - heat: 24%
  - The NHS adds soaps, detergents, fabrics, dust mite, pets, infection, hormones [395].
  - Daily itch follows the weather [394].
  - Lag: heat and sweat on the same day.
- **What helps**
  - Moisturiser (emollient) at least twice a day, **even when skin is clear**.
  - Steroid creams for flares [395].
- **Red flags**
  - Tonight: clusters of painful blisters with fever (possible eczema herpeticum) [396].
  - Tonight: weeping, crusted, hot or quickly spreading skin [395].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes; daily itch is the usual diary measure.
  - The current advice "log foods and skincare" points to the wrong suspects for adults.

## 11. Hair shedding (no daily question)

- **Normal**
  - 50–100 hairs a day [398, 402].
- **Goes with it**
  - Fever, illness, birth, surgery, a crash diet, severe stress or stopping the pill → extra shedding about **3 months later**, lasting 3–6 months [399].
  - After birth it peaks at about 4 months [398].
  - No cause is found in 1 in 3 [399].
  - Iron: mixed evidence. Low ferritin was not more common in women with hair loss than in controls [400, 401].
- **What helps**
  - It usually recovers by itself in 6–9 months [398, 399].
- **Red flags**
  - Soon: not better after 6 months, or patches; blood tests for thyroid and iron are part of the check [399].
- **Ask daily?**
  - **No.** The 3-month lag would make a day-level link blame the wrong days.
  - Better: a monthly "more hair than usual?" check, plus a look back at what happened about 3 months earlier (illness, travel, a hard month).

## 12. Heavy sweating

- **Normal**
  - Hyperhidrosis affects 4.8%; only half have told a professional [403].
- **Goes with it**
  - Primary (no cause) vs secondary (another condition or a medicine) [404].
  - Alcohol and spicy food (advice) [404].
- **What helps**
  - A stronger antiperspirant [404]: aluminium chloride at bedtime on dry skin [406].
  - Loose clothes [404].
- **Red flags**
  - Tonight: sweating with chest pain, light-headedness or nausea [405, 410].
  - Soon: lasting 6+ months, weekly, at night, a sudden increase, or on medicines [404, 405].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes, for people who opt in. Night sweats have their own question.

## 13. Dizziness and light-headedness

- **Goes with it [407]**
  - standing up fast
  - dehydration or heat
  - low sugar (skipped meals)
  - anaemia
  - anxiety
  - inner-ear problems
  - new medicines
  - All on the same day; advice level.
- **What helps**
  - Lie down, get up slowly, drink fluids, less caffeine and alcohol.
  - Don't drive while dizzy [407].
- **Red flags**
  - **Tonight (999)** [408–410]:
    - fainting with chest pain or palpitations
    - fainting during exercise or lying down
    - not coming round
    - face droop, arm weakness or speech trouble, even if it passed
  - Soon: keeps coming back; with hearing, speech or vision changes [407].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes; it is episodic.
  - Dizziness with `heart-palpitations` on the same day → show the safety card once.

**What MeMap can honestly offer:** links with skipped meals, water, heat, heavy period days and medicine changes. These are all common and fixable causes.

## 14. Teeth grinding and jaw pain

- **Normal**
  - Frequent sleep grinding: 13% [412]; pooled 21% [413].
  - Awake clenching: 22–31% [412].
  - The 2018 international consensus calls it a behaviour, not a disorder, in healthy people [411].
- **Goes with it**
  - Stress and anxiety: the most common (advice) [416].
  - Smoking (over 2×), alcohol (about 2×), more than 8 cups of coffee a day (about 1.5×): limited evidence [414].
  - Sleep apnoea; some antidepressants [416].
  - Lag: evening alcohol → morning jaw.
- **What helps**
  - A night guard from a dentist protects the teeth.
  - Relaxation, an ice pack, soft food, no gum [416].
- **Red flags**
  - Tonight: jaw pain with chest pain [410].
  - Same day: new pain when chewing, with headache, tender temples or vision change (giant cell arteritis) [417].
- **Ask daily?**
  - As a morning question: "jaw sore when you woke up?".
  - Self-reported night grinding is unreliable [412].

## 15. Dry eyes (fits the existing `eye-strain`)

- **Normal**
  - 5–50%, depending on the definition; more with age, in women, in Asian people [418].
- **Goes with it**
  - Screens, contact lenses, AC and low humidity, HRT, some medicines (consistent); diabetes, thyroid (probable) [419].
  - Same day.
- **What helps**
  - Pharmacy eye drops, a humidifier, eyelid cleaning, screen breaks [422].
  - **Omega-3 failed** in a 12-month RCT [420].
  - 20-second screen breaks did nothing in a small trial [421].
- **Red flags**
  - Tonight: vision change, light hurting, red eye with headache and nausea [422].
  - Same day: a painful red eye, or a red eye with contact lenses [422].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Yes. The current Russian wording of `eye-strain` includes "двоилось" (double vision). **Sudden double vision is a stroke sign, not something to count** [409].

## 16. Ringing ears (tinnitus)

- **Normal**
  - 14.4% of adults; severe 2.3%. From 9.7% at 18–44 to 23.6% at 65+ [423].
- **Goes with it**
  - Stress and low mood carry the step from "louder" to "more distressing" (cross-sectional) [427].
  - Noise, poor sleep, anxiety (advice) [426].
- **What helps**
  - Information and support, then digital CBT → group → one-to-one.
  - Hearing aids if hearing is down.
  - No medicine is recommended (NICE) [424, 425].
  - Avoid silence; a sleep routine [426].
- **Red flags**
  - Tonight: after a head injury; with sudden hearing loss, face weakness or vertigo [426].
  - Within 24 h: hearing loss that came on over 3 days or less [425].
  - Soon: beating with the pulse; one-sided [425, 426].
- **Ask daily?**
  - Only for people who have it: "louder or more bothersome today?".
  - Repeated tracking did not make it worse [428]. Most app users stop after a day, but many come back [429].

## 17. Fingers that turn white or blue in the cold (Raynaud's)

- **Normal**
  - 4.85% [430]; far more women [432].
- **Goes with it**
  - Cold, sudden temperature change, stress (same day) [431].
  - Smoking: OR 1.27; family history: OR 16.6 [430].
- **What helps**
  - Keep the whole body warm, gloves, exercise, no smoking, less caffeine [431, 432].
- **Red flags (soon) [431, 432]**
  - one side only
  - with joint pain, rash or weakness
  - starting after 30–40
  - in men
  - finger ulcers
- **Ask daily?**
  - In the cold season, for people who opt in. Plain "cold hands" is too vague to help.

## 19. Myths and surprises (for copy and for the founder)

1. **"You should go every day."** Most people don't, and a third of women go less than daily [270]. Normal is 3 a day to 3 a week [268, 269].
2. **"Drink more water for constipation."** It helps only if you drink little, or alongside extra fibre [278, 283, 284].
3. **"Any fibre works."** Psyllium does; wheat bran and inulin are weak, and inulin adds gas [278]. Kiwi works as well as psyllium [282].
4. **"Bad sleep upsets the gut next day."** In a diary study it predicted next-day pain and tiredness, not bowel changes [289]. Stress acts within the same day [292].
5. **"Avoid coffee, citrus and spicy food for heartburn."** There are no elimination trials and coffee evidence is mixed [313–315]. The late dinner is the stronger link [312].
6. **Extra pillows can make reflux worse.** Raise the bed itself [316].
7. **A heart attack can feel like indigestion** [317].
8. **One night-time pee is normal.** Bother starts at 2 [324].
9. **Cutting drinks to stop leaking can backfire** [339].
10. **Avoiding hot-flush triggers, paced breathing and yoga are not recommended** as treatments; placebo helps 20–66% [350].
11. **Evening primrose oil** helps neither breast pain [363] nor hot flushes [350].
12. **ED is an early heart warning** [372, 373].
13. **Omega-3 for dry eyes failed** in a large RCT [420].
14. **Hair shedding lags its cause by about 3 months** [399].
15. **Diaries beat memory**: half of self-described constipated people aren't, by diary [305].

## 20. What this means for the link engine (our inference)

- **"Last night" questions pair with the previous evening's answers**: `night-pee-2-plus`, `night-sweats`, `jaw-sore-morning`. This matches round 1's sleep questions.
- **Same-day pairing**: stress → bowel symptoms [292]; heat or sweat → itch [393]; skipped meal or heat → dizziness [407]; late dinner → heartburn (same night) [312].
- **Next-day pairing**: binge drinking → loose stool [288]; short sleep → desire and sex [380]; night sweats → tiredness [353].
- **Cycle windows** (with `period-day`):
  - bowel changes, worst on day 1 [294]
  - acne in the 7 days before [384]
  - breast tenderness in the 2 weeks before [361]
- **Too slow for day links**: hair shedding (3 months) [399], acne treatment (12 weeks) [387], pelvic floor training (3 months) [342], fibre (4 weeks) [279]. Show these as tries with a start date, not as links.
- **Answers that come together by nature**: heartburn + stomach pain + nausea [321]; bloating + `pms-signs` (which lists bloating); `hard-to-poop` + a "no" on `pooped-today`. Say "often come together", never "we found a link".
- **Follow-up questions** (`heavy-flow-day` on period days; `erection-trouble` and `pain-during-sex` on `had-sex` days) must stay unanswered on other days. If they were answered "no" on other days, the counts would fill with false good days.
